## Mission & Vision

Vision and mission are widely used concepts in business literature to define what a company is doing and, above all, the direction in which it is moving.[^1] Mission and vision statements normally concern the present and future of a company or an endeavor. The mission defines what we are doing today and why[^2], and the vision defines what a company aims to do—the big plan.

## Footnotes

[^1]: If we consider the mission as the coordinates in a Cartesian space that describe a company's position in the market, the vision could be understood as the vector expressing the direction in which the company is moving—or at least plans to move. Wanting to move in a certain direction, while not a sufficient cause to actually move in that direction, is at least a necessary premise for doing so.

[^2]: Simon Sinek popularized the concept of "Start With Why" in his influential 2009 TED talk and subsequent book of the same name. Sinek argues that great leaders and organizations inspire action by communicating why they do what they do—their purpose, cause, or belief—before explaining what they do or how they do it. The "why" sits at the core of a company's mission, defining not just activities but the underlying motivation and meaning that drives those activities. See Simon Sinek, [_Start With Why_](https://simonsinek.com/books/start-with-why/) (2009), and his TED talk ["How Great Leaders Inspire Action"](https://www.ted.com/talks/simon_sinek_how_great_leaders_inspire_action) (2009).
